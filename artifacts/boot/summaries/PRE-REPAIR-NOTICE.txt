WHAT THE `\x09` ACTUALLY WAS.
  The G2 display does not hold ASCII. The display helper at 0x30056FEA is a table-translating
  copy: it loads the per-display table at 0x302A0DE2, indexes it with the SOURCE character, and
  stores the table VALUE into the cell -- never the character. The firmware initialises that table
  to the identity and then deliberately remaps five entries at 0x30056BE8, through a pointer that
  sits 0x2A below the table's character origin:

    moveq #8,%d0   moveb %d0,%a0@(0x91)      0x91 - 0x2A = 0x67 'g' -> 0x08
    moveq #9,%d1   moveb %d1,%a0@(0x9A)      0x9A - 0x2A = 0x70 'p' -> 0x09
    moveq #10,%d2  moveb %d2,%a0@(0x9B)      0x9B - 0x2A = 0x71 'q' -> 0x0A
    moveq #11,%d0  moveb %d0,%a0@(0xA3)      0xA3 - 0x2A = 0x79 'y' -> 0x0B
    moveq #12,%d1  moveb %d1,%a0@(0x94)      0x94 - 0x2A = 0x6A 'j' -> 0x0C

  Those are the five descenders, and 0x08..0x0C is the CGRAM alias range for custom characters
  0..4. The instruction immediately after the fifth store loads 0x300EC4B8, from which the
  firmware uploads the five 8-row glyph bitmaps into those slots. ALL FIVE OFFSETS AGREE ON THE
  SAME 0x2A, so the offset is DERIVED from the five and not fitted to one.

  THE DEFECT WAS IN THE READER, NOT IN THE MACHINE. The test compared raw display cells against
  ASCII, so it mis-read g, j, p, q and y on every G2 display, on every line. `p` is the only
  descender in all thirty-two banner characters, which is why line 0 looked clean and only the
  sixteenth cell of line 1 looked wrong. THE POSITION WAS INCIDENTAL TO THE VALUE; a reader that
  takes those three FAIL lines as a banner-rendering defect, or as a defect at character sixteen,
  is chasing something that was never there.

DO NOT COMPARE AGAINST THESE THREE FILES. There is no post-repair transcript in this directory to
